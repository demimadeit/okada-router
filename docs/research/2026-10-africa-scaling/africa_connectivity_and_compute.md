## Key Question 1: Fibre cuts, outages and network quality (Nigeria and Africa, 2026)

### Takeaway
Nigeria still records thousands of fibre cuts per half-year (5,000+ in H1 2026 per NCC, after 27,000+ in Jan–Nov 2025), terrestrial outages remain dominated by construction damage and power failure rather than submarine cable cuts, and no 2026 cut of WACS/MainOne/SAT-3/2Africa was found. Quality metrics (Ookla ~44 Mbps median mobile in Nigeria; Opensignal: only 18.5% of tests on the best network meet "consistent quality" thresholds) show the problem is variability, not just raw speed.

### Inferences
- The failure mode Okada Router should design for in 2026 is last-mile/metro terrestrial (fibre cuts, power, per-operator reliability) and government shutdowns, not international transit loss; subsea redundancy (2Africa, Equiano) has materially reduced multi-day country-wide outages since 2024.
- Opensignal's "consistent quality" numbers (6.9–18.5% of tests meeting thresholds for demanding apps) are a better proxy for AI-gateway pain than median speed: most sessions on most networks will not sustain a steady streaming connection.
- The apparent drop from 27,000+ cuts (11 months of 2025) to ~5,000 (6 months of 2026) should not be presented as a 60% improvement without caveat; NCC's 2026 statement is framed around construction-linked cuts and a Q1-only figure of 5,934 exists.

- IODA country-level 2026 outage counts for Nigeria/Kenya were not found in search.

## Key Question 2: Power reliability (Nigeria grid 2026, outage economics, generators and solar)

### Takeaway
Nigeria's grid suffered two total collapses in January 2026 (plus one on 29 Dec 2025), none in Q2 2026, then severe partial collapses in August and October 2026; average supply fell from ~4,600 MW (2025) to under 3,500 MW in early 2026. More than 80% of firms report frequent outages, roughly 86% of MSMEs in surveys rely on backup generators, and diesel costs ~₦1,790–2,100/litre, which is pushing a measurable shift to solar (decentralised solar ~6 GW, ~44% of grid capacity).

### Inferences
- The grid trend in 2026 is "fewer total collapses, more partial collapses and chronic load shedding" driven by finance (unpaid Gencos, gas curtailment). For a gateway, this means the device-side power event is a brownout/scheduled cut, not a rare blackout, so local fallback must be cheap to run on inverter/solar.
- Diesel at ~₦1,800–2,100/litre and Band A at ₦225/kWh make the per-token energy cost of a local small model non-trivial on generator power; solar-backed edge hardware is the economic path, consistent with the 6 GW decentralised solar figure.

## Key Question 3: Mobile data pricing, affordability, device and network-generation mix

### Takeaway
Nigeria's regulated 1GB ceiling rose 50% in January 2025 (₦287.50 → ₦431.25) and no further NCC hike was found in 2026; Nigeria is nonetheless among Africa's cheapest markets per GB (~$0.39/GB vs $3.31 SSA average). The binding constraints are device and network generation: 36% of Nigerian connections (68.5 m lines) were still on 2G in mid-2026, 5G is under 5%, SSA smartphone ownership is only 24%, and a smartphone costs 26% of monthly GDP per capita.

## Key Question 6: AI policy, data protection and data-localisation rules relevant to an AI gateway

### Takeaway

Kenya's NAIS 2025–2030, its Dec 2025 implementation roadmap, a 2026 draft AI policy that explicitly covers cloud and compute providers, and a May 2026 draft National Data Governance Policy all push toward localisation but none yet bars offshore processing.

### Inferences
- For Okada Router, the operative Nigerian rule today is the GAID: if queued/cached prompts contain personal data and the fallback target is a US/EU model, the operator is doing a high-risk cross-border transfer that formally needs a DPIA and a transfer instrument or consent. Routing to Cloudflare Lagos / Cassava South Africa / Atlancis Nairobi avoids the "transfer" trigger only for the in-continent path, and South Africa (POPIA) is still a foreign jurisdiction for Nigerian data.
- Design implications: (1) local-model fallback and on-device caching keep prompts in-country and are the compliance-friendly default for health/financial/biometric content; (2) prompt queues should be encrypted at rest with short retention and purge-on-send, since retained prompts are personal data under NDPA; (3) a per-tenant "no-offshore" routing flag maps directly to the CBN payment-data rule (Jan 2027) and to Kenya's expected localisation direction.
