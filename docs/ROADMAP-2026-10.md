# ADTC Closed, But Okada's Niche Stayed Open

**ADTC 2026 is over and Okada Router did not place: the final was pitched on 17 October 2026 and results published on 19 October (1st Agritgllm, 2nd Kuza AI, 3rd Muta), and Okada is not on the ten-finalist list ([ADTC results](https://adtc.substack.com/p/africa-deep-tech-challenge-2026-results); [ADTC finalists](https://adtc.substack.com/p/the-adtc-2026-finalists-are-in-now)).** That closes the prize track but not the opportunity, because the two months since Gate 1 moved the landscape in Okada's favour on substance while raising the bar on hygiene. The gateway market consolidated around hosted, server-side routers (Stripe agreed to buy OpenRouter for roughly $7B; Portkey is inside Palo Alto Networks; Helicone is in maintenance mode) and none of them added client-side network sensing or offline fallback, so Okada's differentiation is intact ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/19/stripe-openrouter-fintech-ai-model-marketplace-.html); [awesome-ai-gateway](https://github.com/cuihuan/awesome-ai-gateway)). At the same time, the open-source leader LiteLLM shipped a compromised release 1.107.0 on 24 October 2026, Groq retired free-tier models twice with about a month's notice, and llama.cpp v0.7.0 moved llama-server's default port from 8080 to 9931, each of which touches Okada's code directly ([LiteLLM issue #47826](https://github.com/BerriAI/litellm/issues/47826); [Groq deprecations](https://console.groq.com/docs/deprecations); [llama.cpp v0.7.0](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/releases/tag/v0.7.0)). The direct answers to the founder's questions are: you need API keys only for the cloud rungs and a pilot can run on stacked free tiers; you do not need to train a model to ship, and the first "training" worth doing is a telemetry schema change, not a GPU job; the realistic twelve-month outcome for one unfunded person is a hardened open-source gateway with a few hundred installs, one to three paid on-prem compliance deployments driven by Nigeria's 1 January 2027 CBN payment-data localisation deadline, and one grant-funded vertical pilot with published metrics, which adds up to low tens of thousands of dollars, not a Starlink-scale business ([tech.africa](https://tech.africa/?p=88017)). Before any of that, four headline figures in the README, whitepaper and ADTC report need correcting, starting with the "~190 days/year grid downtime" claim, which this research could not source anywhere.

## What changed between 25 August and 25 October 2026

The competitive picture hardened in Okada's favour. Stripe announced its agreement to acquire OpenRouter on 19 August 2026 with the price reported at roughly **$7–7.5B**, Kong shipped AI Gateway 2.0 and 2.2, Cloudflare unified Workers AI and AI Gateway billing and added BYOK enforcement and x402 machine payments, and TensorZero, Pydantic AI Gateway and (effectively) RouteLLM went dormant ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/19/stripe-openrouter-fintech-ai-model-marketplace-.html); [Cloudflare AI Gateway changelog](https://developers.cloudflare.com/ai-gateway/changelog/); [awesome-ai-gateway](https://github.com/cuihuan/awesome-ai-gateway)). Every one of these routes on provider-side signals from a server the client must first reach; the only "local fallback" projects found (Swobu, RelayPlane, Olla, OSW, Ollama Herd, offline-chat-shell, llm-queue) route among local backends or do priority failover and none measures network conditions ([awesome-ai-gateway](https://github.com/cuihuan/awesome-ai-gateway); [offline-chat-shell](https://github.com/noumanshakeil/offline-chat-shell)). The research found no paper or product combining semantic caching with intermittent-connectivity gateways and no "store-and-forward for LLM requests" standard, so Okada's queue-and-sync design has no reference implementation it must match ([LaCache, arXiv 2608.01718](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01718)). The LiteLLM compromise is the sharpest lesson of the window: release 1.107.0 carried a credential stealer and a `sitecustomize` loader built from a lookalike "Berri-AI/litellm" repository, the PyPI release and Docker `latest` tags were pulled on 24 October, and this followed a March 2026 PyPI compromise that had already forced LiteLLM onto an isolated CI pipeline ([LiteLLM issue #47826](https://github.com/BerriAI/litellm/issues/47826); [futureagi](https://futureagi.com/blog/best-ai-gateways-aider-local-models-2026/)). Okada's six-line `requirements.txt` is a selling point only if it is pinned; today it is a set of `>=` ranges and a floating `ghcr.io/ggml-org/llama.cpp:server` image tag.

The cloud rungs moved under Okada's feet. Groq shut down `llama-3.1-8b-instant` and `llama-3.3-70b-versatile` on 16 August 2026 and retired `qwen/qwen3.6-27b` on 14 September in favour of the pricier `qwen3.8-27b` ($0.80/$4.00 vs $0.60/$3.00 per million tokens); Okada's configured `openai/gpt-oss-120b` and `gpt-oss-20b` survived both waves and are Groq's own recommended replacements, with free-tier limits for 120b reported at **30 RPM, 1,000 RPD, 8,000 TPM and 200,000 TPD** and paid pricing at $0.15/$0.60 per million tokens ([Groq deprecations](https://console.groq.com/docs/deprecations); [free-model.com](https://www.free-model.com/models/groq/openai-gpt-oss-120b-2/); [glamdring](https://www.glamdringresearch.com/post/groq-pricing)). The operational detail that matters for Okada's code is a developer report that a retired Groq model returned "model does not exist" while a health check kept reporting OK because it never issued a real completion ([dhseadev](https://dhseadev.online/2026/09/21/groq-retired-the-model-health-check-said-ok/)); Okada's `network/health.py` does exactly that, probing `GET {base_url}/models` and recording any status below 500 as healthy. On the pricing side, Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite is free of charge on the free tier including thinking tokens and $0.30/$2.50 paid, Claude Haiku 5.5 was reported on 7 October at $0.10/$0.50 per million (third-party only; the official page could not be fetched), and OpenAI's cheapest tier naming conflicts across trackers ([Gemini pricing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing); [dev.to roundup](https://dev.to/freetierkits/cheapest-llm-apis-per-1m-tokens-oct-2026-20-models-compared-plus-free-tiers-4mn9)). Okada's `config.yaml` still names `gemini-2.5-flash`, `claude-sonnet-5` and `claude-haiku-4-5-20251001`, which need re-verification against live model lists.

The local rung got faster and changed shape. llama.cpp shipped v0.6.0 (5 October) and v0.7.0 (15 October) with CPU-relevant wins (tiled mul_mat for k-quants, Q2_0 AVX2 kernels, faster model loading, direct-io) and new speculative-decoding modes (`--spec-type draft-mtp`, `draft-dflash`, `ngram-*`), but also breaking server changes: the **default port moved from 8080 to 9931**, formatted chat output became an array of parts, prompt-cache and slot semantics changed, and session-file versions bumped ([llama.cpp v0.6.0](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/releases/tag/v0.6.0); [llama.cpp v0.7.0](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/releases/tag/v0.7.0); [llama-server README](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/tools/server/README.md)). On models, nothing new under 10B shipped from Qwen, Google, Meta or OpenAI in the window (Qwen3.8 is 27B and 2.4T only), so Qwen3.5 0.8B/2B/4B/9B from March 2026 remains the current small line and a drop-in GGUF upgrade from Okada's Qwen2.5-1.5B, with the bonus that Qwen3.5 carries an MTP head llama.cpp can use for speculative decoding ([QwenLM Qwen3.5 README](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3.5); [Ollama v0.32.6](https://github.com/ollama/ollama/releases?page=4)). The one genuinely new small model is NVIDIA's Nemotron 3.5 Bolt E4B (23 October), a Gemma 4 E4B fine-tune shipping with a DFlash draft and GGUFs, scoring 63.2 vs 38.7 on Tau2-Bench against base Gemma 4 E4B ([Ollama v0.40.10](https://github.com/ollama/ollama/releases/tag/v0.40.10); [Unsloth v0.1.910-beta](https://github.com/unslothai/unsloth/releases/tag/v0.1.910-beta)). Ollama itself drifted toward MLX, cloud sign-in and desktop integrations across v0.32 to v0.40, which makes llama-server the more predictable offline dependency even though Ollama's OpenAI-compatible streaming now matches OpenAI's format ([Ollama releases](https://github.com/ollama/ollama/releases)).

The African infrastructure picture shifted in three ways that bear on positioning. Meta began charging per utility message inside the WhatsApp 24-hour service window on 1 October 2026 (Kenya about $0.0040, Nigeria about $0.0101 per message, figures conflicting across outlets), which raises the running cost of every Farmer.Chat- and Rori-style bot and makes a cache-and-local-fallback layer worth money to them ([Business Daily Africa](https://www.businessdailyafrica.com/bd/markets/market-news/meta-sets-whatsapp-messaging-charges-5573670); [Legit.ng](https://www.legit.ng/business-economy/technology/1727481-whatsapp-charge-nigerian-businesses-message-october-1-what-companies-know/)). In-continent inference moved from slideware to partial reality: Cassava's Nvidia AI Factory went live in Cape Town with 3,000 GPUs and announced on 21 October that it will serve Nvidia Nemotron open models as a managed service, and the World Bank announced a shared pay-as-you-go GPU initiative for Africa and Latin America on 20 October with Cassava and Nvidia as partners, though nothing is live in Nigeria or Kenya and frontier closed models still terminate in the US and EU even when called through Cape Town, Johannesburg or South Africa North ([Techweez](https://techweez.com/2026/10/21/cassava-ai-factory-nvidia/); [Ecofin Agency](https://www.ecofinagency.com/news-digital/2010-59955-world-bank-launches-shared-ai-computing-initiative-for-africa-latin-america); [AWS Bedrock regional availability](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/models-region-compatibility.html)). And the grid did what Okada's pitch says it does: after zero collapses in Q2 2026, partial collapses on 22 August (generation to 1,132 MW) and 19–20 October (to 675 MW or 476.7 MW depending on the outlet) put the outage story back in the news ([ThisDay](https://www.thisdaylive.com/2026/08/23/discos-embark-on-load-shedding-as-power-grid-suffers-partial-collapse-generation-plunges-to-1132mw/); [allAfrica](https://allafrica.com/stories/202610200396.html); [TheFact Daily](https://thefact.ng/nigerias-power-sector-recorded-zero-grid-collapse-in-q2-2026-nerc/)).

The ADTC report additionally still asserts "no frontier-model inference capacity anywhere on the continent", a claim the whitepaper withdrew after external review; the two documents now contradict each other.

## Keys yes, training no: the founder's direct questions answered

**API keys.** The cloud rungs need keys; the local and queue rungs need none. A pilot can run for $0 by stacking free tiers behind Okada's existing failover: Groq gpt-oss-120b at roughly 1,000 requests/day, OpenRouter `:free` models at 20 RPM and 50 requests/day rising to 1,000/day once the account has ever bought $10 of credit, Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite free (its daily cap is disputed between ~1,500 and ~100 RPD across sources), Cloudflare Workers AI at 10,000 Neurons/day, and SambaNova at 20 RPD ([OpenRouter pricing](https://openrouter.ai/pricing); [OpenRouter rate-limit help](https://openrouter.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/39501163636379-OpenRouter-Rate-Limits-What-You-Need-to-Know); [Gemini pricing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing); [aireiter quota comparison](https://aireiter.com/blog/free-llm-api-quota-comparison)). All of these limits are secondary-sourced because the official pages were unreachable, and they are enough for one shop or one clinic, not a fleet. The right product design is hybrid: operator-pooled upstream keys with per-tenant virtual keys and hard daily budgets (the LiteLLM pattern of `max_budget` and `budget_duration`), plus BYO keys as a per-tenant override for NGOs and enterprises that already have accounts ([elest.io LiteLLM guide](https://blog.elest.io/litellm-stop-burning-money-on-llm-apis-virtual-keys-cost-tracking-and-guardrails/)). Two things make pooled keys a real product in Nigeria rather than a convenience: USD-billed APIs "routinely reject African cards", some banks cap international naira-card spend around $500/month, and Nigerian developers report unresolved OpenAI card declines, so a gateway that is paid in naira via Paystack or Flutterwave and meters a pooled key is a wedge no comparable offers ([Global Fintech Series](https://globalfintechseries.com/banking/digital-payments/africa-leads-the-world-in-ai-adoption-but-cant-pay-for-it-linkpay-brings-instant-virtual-cards-for-ai-services/); [TechCabal](https://techcabal.com/es/?p=165651); [OpenAI Community](https://community.openai.com/t/how-do-i-make-payement-for-api-from-nigeria-my-card-is-constantly-been-declined/567647)). Budgets mean nothing until the telemetry cost table is real: `COST_PER_M` in `gateway/telemetry.py` hardcodes four OpenAI and Anthropic prices and returns zero for every Groq and Gemini model Okada actually routes to.

**Training.** Nothing in the gateway requires training. Routing is rule-based on network probes and the local model is an off-the-shelf GGUF. The three places training could add value are a learned routing policy, a language adapter and distillation of cloud answers into the local model, and all three are blocked by the same thing: Okada's telemetry records network state, decision, attempts, bytes, tokens and latency but **no prompt text and no quality label**. RouteLLM's routers were trained on prompt-plus-pairwise-preference data from Chatbot Arena, augmented with golden labels and LLM-judge scores, to reach 95% of GPT-4 quality on 26% of GPT-4 calls; Okada has zero labelled routing outcomes, so step one is a schema change (hashed prompt, language ID, an explicit or implicit quality signal such as "user re-asked within 60 seconds"), and a learned router becomes worth building only after roughly ten thousand labelled outcomes exist ([RouteLLM arXiv 2406.18665](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.18665v2)). The cheapest useful training is a QLoRA language adapter on a 1.5–3B base over Inkuba-Instruct, AfriQA (12,000+ cross-lingual QA pairs in 10 African languages) and AfriSenti (110,000+ tweets in 14 languages including Hausa, Igbo, Yoruba and Nigerian Pidgin); at RunPod's $1.19/hour A100 and a third-party estimate of about 20 hours for a 7B QLoRA over 100k examples, a 3B pass is in the **$5–40 range of GPU time**, with data cleaning and evaluation as the real cost ([AfriQA](https://www.huggingface.co/datasets/masakhane/afriqa/blob/e27efe56b71d639f6713c1287aeba1753afaea62/README.md); [AfriSenti](https://huggingface.co/datasets/HausaNLP/AfriSenti-Twitter); [RunPod pricing](https://www.runpod.io/gpu-cloud/pricing); [deploybase](https://deploybase.ai/articles/a100-on-runpod-pricing-specs-how-to-rent)). Distillation becomes feasible only after opt-in, consented prompt logging exists; community recipes used about 96,000 teacher-student pairs ([Featherless model page](https://featherless.ai/models/huggingFacing/qwen2.5-7b-to-1.5b-gkd-paper100k-step1500-seed10)).

**Which languages a small local model can serve.** The honest reading of the benchmarks is that 1B-class models should be treated as English-and-Swahili-only and that 3–4B is the floor for Hausa, Yoruba and Igbo. NVIDIA's Gemma 3 model cards show the multilingual cliff between sizes: MGSM 2.04 at 1B vs 34.7 at 4B, Global-MMLU-Lite 24.9 vs 57.0 ([NVIDIA Gemma 3 1B card](https://docs.api.nvidia.com/nim/reference/google-gemma-3-1b-it)). IrokoBench found the best open model (Gemma 2 27B) reached only 63% of GPT-4o across 17 African languages and that translate-test (machine-translate to English first) helps English-centric models, which suggests a practical architecture of a small MT step into English, the English-centric small model, then MT back, rather than hoping a 1.5B model reasons in Yoruba ([IrokoBench arXiv 2406.03368](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.03368)). Every successful African deployment got language support from continued pretraining of a 7–8B model on hundreds of millions of local-language tokens (Jacaranda's UlizaLlama on 321,530,045 Swahili tokens; Lugha-Llama on the WURA corpus), not from a 1B model ([UlizaLlama](https://huggingface.co/Jacaranda/UlizaLlama/blob/6775e74bddd064bae0aef38f93423f52bb0fd00a/README.md?code=true); [Lugha-Llama arXiv 2504.06536](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.06536)). Nigeria's own N-ATLAS (Llama-3 8B on 400M+ tokens of Yoruba, Hausa, Igbo and Nigerian English, launched 20 September 2025) is free for research and deployments under 1,000 end users and requires a licence above that; integrating it as an optional local rung is a cheap way to open a conversation with NITDA/NCAIR ([TechCabal](https://techcabal.com/2025/09/25/nigerian-government-awarri-launch-n-atlas/)). No vendor released an African-language small model in the August–October window ([AfriNLLB](https://awesomepapers.io/natural-language-processing/papers/2602.09373); [MzansiLM](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.20732v1)).

## Hardware: an RK3588 board beats a Pi 5, and nobody should manufacture anything

For the "Okada box" that a pharmacy, clinic or school would run, the measured 2026 numbers rank an RK3588 board first. llama.cpp on the RK3588 CPU runs Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct Q4_K_M at **22.57 tokens/s**, and the RK3588's NPU is no faster than its CPU at this model size (RKLLM runs of 1.5–1.8B models land at 14–15 tok/s), so the NPU-HAT hype can be ignored for small LLMs ([Turing Pi RK3588 benchmarks](https://turingpi.com/llm-inference-benchmarks-rk3588-gguf-quantization/); [CNX RK3588 DeepSeek](https://cnx-software.com/2025/02/09/deepseek-rockchip-rk3588-npu-ai-acceleration-15-tokens-per-second)). A Raspberry Pi 5 serves the same model at about **10 tok/s** using 1.85 GB RAM, throttles to 3–4 tok/s without a cooler, and its 16 GB variant jumped $60 to **$205** in February 2026 on RAM shortages, which makes it hard to justify against an RK3588 board ([Stratosphere Pi 5 study](https://stratospherelinuxips.readthedocs.io/en/develop/immune/research_rpi_llm_performance.html); [hackernoon](https://hackernoon.com/self-hosting-ai-models-on-a-raspberry-pi-5-a-complete-guide-to-free-private-local-ai-inference); [PCWorld](https://www.pcworld.com/article/3049682/raspberry-pi-prices-leap-again-as-ram-shortages-slam-pcs.html)). The Jetson Orin Nano Super is the only option that comfortably serves the 3–4B models the language floor demands, at **43 tok/s on Llama 3.2 3B INT4** and 240 tok/s aggregate across 8 parallel streams under vLLM, but it reportedly moved from $249 to $399 in July 2026 ([Jetson AI Lab benchmarks](https://jetson-ai-lab.com/benchmarks.html); [computingforgeeks](https://computingforgeeks.com/edge-llm-jetson-orin-vs-ai-hat-2-vs-hailo-10h/)). Power is the solar-friendly part: a Pi-class board idles at 2.7–3 W and draws 8–9 W during inference, so a 100 W panel and a 600 Wh battery runs a box plus modem through a day of load shedding by simple arithmetic, which matters when diesel is ₦1,790–2,100 per litre and 86% of Nigerian MSMEs run generators ([linuxlinks power table](https://www.linuxlinks.com/raspberry-pi5-mini-pc-power-consumption/3/); [GlobalPetrolPrices](https://www.globalpetrolprices.com/Nigeria/diesel_prices/); [ZE-Gen](https://www.ze-gen.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/Ze-Gen_Understanding-Nigerias-Fossil-Fuel-Generator-Challenge.pdf)). No Nigeria or Kenya retail prices for any of these boards surfaced, and no llama-bench numbers exist for an N100 mini-PC or a used office i5, so a local measurement run is needed before any price list.

The cautionary tales are specific. BRCK, the Nairobi rugged-router company, is listed as no longer operating; its device is out of production and its Facebook-funded free Wi-Fi business "was wiped out by the Covid-19 wave" ([Unreasonable Group](https://unreasonablegroup.com/ventures/brck); [Business Daily Africa](https://www.businessdailyafrica.com/bd/corporate/technology/why-six-kenyan-promising-tech-start-ups-fell-3987374)). Mara Phones' Durban factory was shut when its funder pulled the plug over missed production targets ([Africa.com](https://africa.com/top-10-news/a-promising-venture-for-a-made-in-south-africa-smartphone-dashed)). The box should therefore be an installer image on hardware the customer already owns or an NGO funds (the Juza AI route: solar-powered offline classroom tutors in Samburu, Isiolo and Garissa under a Mastercard Foundation fellowship), and llamafile's single-binary approach is worth copying as a distribution format ([iHub directory](https://futureoflearning.ihub.co.ke/startup-directory/juza-ai/)).

Phones are a harder ceiling than boxes. Tecno, Infinix and itel hold roughly 42% of Nigerian usage share, entry-level 2026 models ship with 3–4 GB RAM, and Africa's sub-$100 smartphone segment shrank 34% in Q2 2026 on memory costs, so the installed base will skew low-RAM for years ([Statcounter Nigeria](https://gs.statcounter.com/vendor-market-share/mobile-/nigeria); [Omdia](https://omdia.tech.informa.com/pr/2026/aug/africa-smartphone-market-expected-to-decline-26percent-in-2026-following-first-contraction-in-three-years); [NaijaTechGuide](https://www.naijatechguide.com/infinix-phones-prices-features-specs.html)). The measured on-device numbers are flagship-only: Gemma 3n E2B decodes at 16.1 tok/s on a Samsung S24 Ultra CPU, Llama 3.2 1B Q4 reaches 45.69 tok/s on a Snapdragon Hexagon NPU, and MediaPipe's LLM API is "optimized for high-end Android devices such as Pixel 8 and Samsung S23 or later", while Gemini Nano 4 reportedly needs 12 GB RAM and Apple's on-device model supports no African language ([Gemma 3n LiteRT card](https://huggingface.co/google/gemma-3n-E2B-it-litert-lm/blob/refs%2Fpr%2F2/README.md); [llama.cpp Snapdragon README](https://huggingface.co/datasets/echodict/llama.cpp/blob/main/docs/backend/snapdragon/README.md); [MediaPipe LLM Inference](https://developers.google.com/edge/mediapipe/solutions/genai/llm_inference/android); [ninetwothree](https://www.ninetwothree.co/blog/gemini-nano-4-device-support)). Thermal measurements show iPhone power spikes above 18 W and 10–20% degradation over five consecutive runs, and a Greenspector CPU test saw 2–7 tok/s with throttling variance ([MELTing Point](https://www.alphaxiv.org/overview/2403.12844); [arXiv 2603.23640](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.23640v1); [Greenspector](https://blog.greenspector.com/en/environmental-impact-of-local-ai/)). The practical SDK is therefore thin: llama.cpp via JNI or llama.rn with the same GGUF artefacts as the box, a policy that prefers the LAN box, caps local generation around 200 tokens, stops after N consecutive local answers when hot or below 20% battery, and distributes model weights over LAN or Wi-Fi Direct rather than a 700 MB mobile download. Sharding a model across a shop's phones is not realistic: exo's fast path assumes Thunderbolt 5 RDMA and decode is memory-bandwidth-bound ([CNX exo](https://www.cnx-software.com/2025/02/18/exo-software-a-distributed-llm-solution-running-on-a-cluster-of-computers-smartphones-or-sbcs/)). The Airtel Africa–SpaceX direct-to-cell service, piloted in Kenya in March 2026 and still awaiting regulator approval in August, is scoped to text, USSD, WhatsApp and mobile money, almost exactly the envelope of a compressed prompt and response, and is a plausible last-resort transport in 2027 ([Techweez](https://techweez.com/2026/03/25/airtel-starlink-direct-to-cell/); [TechTrendsKE](https://techtrendske.co.ke/2026/08/04/airtel-kenya-satellite-to-phone-service/)).

## Eight avenues, three of them realistic for one unfunded founder

The gateway category's economics argue against "another gateway SaaS": Portkey's entry tier is $49/month plus $9 per 100K logs, Helicone Pro is $79/month with 16,000 organisations over three years before its acqui-hire, and LiteLLM's enterprise pricing is sales-led, so the surviving pattern is open-source core plus hosted convenience plus enterprise features such as SSO, audit logs and SLAs ([Eden AI](https://www.edenai.co/post/best-portkey-alternatives-7-ai-gateways-compared); [Mintlify blog](https://www.mintlify.com/blog/mintlify-acquires-helicone)). Nobody found in the research sells a gateway in naira, and nobody sells "prompts never leave Nigeria" as a feature, which is where the compliance avenue comes from. The CBN circular of June 2026 requires payment transaction data to be stored in Nigeria by **1 January 2027** for banks, fintechs, mobile-money operators and PSPs; the GAID 2025 (effective 19 September 2025) classes finance and health as "extra-high-level" sectors requiring annual compliance audit returns, makes DPIAs mandatory for automated decision-making and treats cross-border transfer as high-risk processing, and the NDPC had published no adequacy decisions as of August 2026 ([tech.africa](https://tech.africa/?p=88017); [Mondaq fintech localisation](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/fin-tech/1722826/how-nigerias-data-localization-regime-shapes-fintechs-handling-of-financial-identity-and-transaction-data); [Legal 500 cross-border guide](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/nigeria/privacy/cross-border-data-transfers-under-nigerias-ndpa-a-compliance-guide); [Omaplex](https://omaplex.com.ng/critical-assessment-of-the-nigerian-data-protection-act-general-application-and-implementation-directive-2025/)). A gateway that keeps cache, queue and telemetry on Nigerian soil, strips PII before any cloud call and offers a per-tenant "no-offshore" routing flag is a direct answer to both, but the README admits the cache has no PII handling and nothing is encrypted at rest, so this is a build item before it can be sold.

The precedents say channel beats model and infrastructure is the gap. Jacaranda Health's PROMPTS reaches 95,000+ mothers in Ghana over SMS with an AI helpdesk on UlizaLlama; Digital Green's Farmer.Chat answered 165K questions in FY25 with 70% of interactions in local languages, serves over 1 million users across India, Kenya, Ethiopia, Nigeria and Brazil, and fine-tuned GPT-4o Mini rather than paying for frontier models; Rori, the Ghanaian WhatsApp maths tutor, showed an effect size of 0.36 at about $5 per student; Ask Viamo Anything delivered 93% valid answers to its first 500 voice users with more than 60% of interactions from women ([Digital Green FY25 report](https://digitalgreen.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Digital-Green-FY25-Annual-Report.pdf); [Digital Green + OpenAI](https://www.digitalgreen.org/insights/digital-green-openai-farmerchat); [Stanford SCALE on Rori](https://scale.stanford.edu/publications/effective-and-scalable-math-support-experimental-evidence-impact-ai-math-tutor-ghana); [GSMA on Viamo](https://www.gsma.com/solutions-and-impact/connectivity-for-good/mobile-for-development/gsma_resources/voice-first-generative-ai-for-impact-insights-from-viamos-ask-viamo-anything-pilot-in-zambia/)). None of them runs a model on-prem or on the user's device; "offline" for them means the channel tolerates disconnection, and all of them now pay Meta per WhatsApp utility message. Okada is the layer they lack, and the ADTC 2026 winners (two offline farm advisors and a tutor) are the same kind of vertical app that needs a hybrid online/offline backend ([ADTC results](https://adtc.substack.com/p/africa-deep-tech-challenge-2026-results)). The fastest route to relevance is to be the backend for a PROMPTS- or Farmer.Chat-style WhatsApp bot and report "answered rate during outage" as the KPI. On the "next Starlink" ambition: Starlink itself, with SpaceX's balance sheet, has 98,642 Nigerian subscribers after roughly three years, is second to Spectranet, is sold out in parts of Lagos and Abuja, and froze new residential orders in seven Kenyan counties in July 2026; ubiquity in Africa historically came from telco-bundled distribution (Opera Mini) and basic-phone channels (Viamo), so the Starlink-like outcome for Okada is "default resilience layer inside other people's apps and NGO deployments", reached through open-source distribution ([Space in Africa](https://spaceinafrica.com/2026/08/26/starlink-narrows-the-gap-to-under-13000-as-nigerias-isp-market-fragments-further-in-q2-2026/); [TechCabal on Kenya freeze](https://techcabal.com/2026/07/07/starlink-stops-new-orders-in-7-parts-of-kenya/)).

The targets to hold yourself to are modest and the research supports them: revenue in the low tens of thousands of USD from one to three on-prem deployments (naira enterprise licences have no public comparables; the ₦1–5m/year anchor is inferred) plus one grant-funded pilot, rather than SaaS MRR, which PostHog needed venture capital and roughly twelve months to reach even with a US developer market ([BusinessWire on PostHog](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20201217006165/en/PostHog-Raises-%2412-Million-in-Funding-Led-by-GV-and-Y-Combinator-to-Bring-Open-Source-Product-Analytics-Platform-to-Engineers)). What needs capital: manufacturing, Lagos GPU hosting at scale (Kasi Cloud's H100/H200 campus exists but rates were not found, and operators face a 64% energy-cost rise since January), and multi-country rollout ([TechCabal on Lagos compute](https://techcabal.com/2026/01/08/will-nigeria-get-its-first-ai-data-centre-in-2026/)). Three uncertainties could change the plan and should be re-checked on the official pages before acting: every free-tier limit cited here is secondary-sourced and some conflict; the CBN circular text itself could not be fetched; and whether Okada made the ADTC 20-semifinalist list could not be confirmed, which matters only for how the feedback email is worded.

## Conclusion

The two months since Gate 1 did not close Okada's window; they clarified what it is for. The gateway market's consolidation into hosted, server-side routers and the LiteLLM compromise together mean the market will reward a small, pinned, auditable gateway that works when the client cannot reach anyone, and nothing that shipped in the window competes on that axis. The strongest new argument is regulatory rather than technical: Nigeria's CBN payment-data deadline of 1 January 2027 and the GAID's high-risk classification of cross-border transfers turn "prompts, cache and queue never leave Nigeria" from a slogan into a dated compliance feature, and Okada is a few weeks of security work away from being able to sell it, which is more than any comparable can say.

The sharper implication is that Okada should stop describing itself as a product people buy and start behaving as the layer other people's products run on. Every successful African AI deployment the research found chose a lowest-common-denominator channel, put the model in the cloud and measured outcomes, and every one of them now pays per WhatsApp message and loses answers during fibre cuts; the ADTC winners are vertical apps with no hybrid backend. The founder's leverage is to become the measured, naira-billed, in-country resilience layer under those apps, prove it with answered-rate numbers from real MTN and Airtel links rather than simulated profiles, and let the dataset that falls out of that position be the moat the whitepaper already describes.
